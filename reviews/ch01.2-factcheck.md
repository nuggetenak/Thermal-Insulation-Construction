## The question this review was called to answer

One briefing pack fed all five items. If the pack misread a clause, five items
carry the same error and agree with each other perfectly.

**The pack is mostly accurate, and where it is wrong the items inherited it.**
Section by section: the 安衛法 and 安衛則 summaries in A and B are faithful; the
specification summaries in C are faithful; the 要綱 summaries in D are faithful
and the volume warning in D is correct and was obeyed. **Section E, on waste
law, is where it fails**, and section B fails by omission in a way that shaped
the whole of `01.2.05`.

Two errors are serious enough to lead the report:

- **The pack, the registry note and `01.2.04` all omit the exclusion of
  特別管理廃棄物 from the subcontractor-carriage test** (施行規則第18条の2第1項第1号).
  For a trade that strips old lagging, 廃石綿等 is the exclusion most likely to
  bite, and the item presents its condition list as complete (finding 1).
- **The pack, and therefore `01.2.05`, missed 安衛則第620条** — a duty written
  directly on the worker to keep the work area clean and not to dump waste
  outside the designated place. `01.2.05` is the housekeeping item, its thesis
  is "part of that expectation is a legal duty rather than a courtesy", and it
  concludes that the daily standard is trade practice rather than law. The
  clause that makes it law sits one article after 第619条, which the item does
  discuss (finding 2).

Beyond those, `01.2.04`'s account of the subcontractor-carriage test has five
further defects, all in the direction that would let a reader think they
qualify when they do not (findings 3 to 7). The other four items are
substantially sound: their clause attributions are right, their numbers are
right, their readings are right, and the three deliberate scope limits are all
defensible, though one of them (第619条) is right for the wrong reason. The
recurring weakness across all five is **scope inflation** — a clause applied
slightly wider than it reaches, four times over (findings 9, 11, 13, 16).

## Findings

### 1. `01.2.04`, the pack and the registry — the 特別管理廃棄物 exclusion is missing from the subcontractor-carriage test

**Claim.** "It requires a **written term in the subcontract** allowing it, and
then every one of the following to hold at once: the work is not demolition,
and not new build or extension work; its contract value is 5,000,000 yen or
less…"

**What the source says.** 施行規則第18条の2第1項第1号 opens by defining the
waste as 「建設工事に伴い生ずる廃棄物（**特別管理一般廃棄物及び特別管理産業廃棄物を除く**。…）」.
The exclusion of specially controlled waste is a condition of the exception, on
the same footing as the ¥5,000,000 limit and the 1 m³ limit. Neither the item,
nor the pack (section E), nor the registry note for `haikibutsu-shoriho`
mentions it. Confirmed against the verbatim text and independently against
Miyagi Prefecture's guidance page on the 下請負人による運搬の特例.

Why this one and not another omission: 廃石綿等 — waste asbestos and material
contaminated with it — is 特別管理産業廃棄物. Stripping old thermal insulation
is exactly where a member of this trade meets it. An item that tells a reader
the test is complete, and omits the one exclusion their trade is most likely to
trip, is worse than an item that gives no list.

**Severity: critical.**

**Suggested action.** Add the exclusion to the item's condition list, to the
pack and to the registry note. Give it its own sentence naming 廃石綿等, and
cross-reference the asbestos material wherever it lives.

### 2. `01.2.05` and the pack — 安衛則第620条 puts the daily housekeeping duty on the worker, and neither knows it exists

**Claim.** "The daily habit this item is really about — leaving a ward corridor
walkable every evening, not only on the last day of the job — is **trade
practice** built on top of 1.3.8(3) and on the Act's 第23条, not something
1.3.11 itself requires."

**What the source says.** 安衛則第620条, captioned 労働者の清潔保持義務 and
sitting immediately after the 第619条 the item does discuss, in the same
第三編第七章 清潔:

> 労働者は、作業場の清潔に注意し、廃棄物を定められた場所以外の場所にすてない
> ようにしなければならない。

A duty on the worker — not the employer — to attend to the cleanliness of the
work area and not to discard waste anywhere other than the designated place.
That is the item's entire subject, written as a legal obligation on the person
reading the item. It is also directly on point for `01.2.04`'s waste-zone
subject, which likewise does not cite it.

The pack's section B lists 第619条 with a scope caution and moves straight to
第635条. 第620条 is not in the pack at all. The item's negative claim — that the
daily standard is trade practice rather than law — is a reasonable conclusion
from the pack and wrong against the regulations.

This is the failure mode `reviews/ch01-factcheck.md` finding 3 named:
understating a rule leaves the reader not applying a rule that binds them.
Here it is worse than understatement, because the item states positively that
no such duty exists.

**Severity: critical.**

**Suggested action.** Add 第620条 to the pack, to the `mhlw-anzen` registry
note, and to `01.2.05` as the legal root of the daily standard — it is a better
root for this item than 第23条, because 第23条 binds the employer and 第620条
binds the reader. Rewrite the "trade practice" sentence. Add it to `01.2.04`'s
waste section too: "do not put waste anywhere except the designated place" is
a clause, not advice. Check the other four items for the same absence.

### 3. `01.2.04` — "segregated by type" misreads 区分して運搬

**Claim.** "each load carried is 1 cubic metre or less, **kept segregated by
type so that this is evident on sight**"

**What the source says.** 施行規則第18条の2第1項第2号イ:
「一回当たりに運搬される量が一立方メートル以下であることが明らかとなるよう
**区分して**運搬されるもの」. The 区分 is a division that makes the **volume per
trip** evident — loads kept separated so that no single trip exceeds 1 m³. It
is not segregation by waste type. Kanagawa Prefecture's guidance renders the
same condition as being able to show 1 m³ or less by a simple method such as a
tape measure, or by using a container that obviously holds no more.

The pack got this right — "each load is 1 cubic metre or less, and segregated
so that this is evident". The words "by type" are the item's own addition, and
they almost certainly leaked in from 1.3.9's 分別, which the same item
translates as "segregated by type" three paragraphs later. 分別 and 区分 are
different words doing different work, and the item has merged them.

**Severity: significant.** A reader who sorts offcuts by material and loads
2 m³ of them has satisfied the condition the item states and breached the
condition the rule states.

**Suggested action.** Drop "by type". State that the requirement is that the
1 m³ per trip be demonstrable.

### 4. `01.2.04` — the ¥5,000,000 threshold does not say whose contract, and the ambiguity runs the dangerous way

**Claim.** "its contract value is 5,000,000 yen or less"

**What the source says.** 施行規則第18条の2第1項第1号イ:
「建設工事（…）であつて、その**請負代金の額**が五百万円以下であるもの」. The
clause attaches the amount to the 建設工事 — and the 建設工事 whose 元請業者 is
the 事業者 under 第21条の3第1項 is the job as taken from the client.
Secondary guidance is explicit: 行政書士吉田哲朗事務所's clause commentary
renders it 「**元請負代金**が500万円以下」, and Kanagawa's guidance frames it as
the 維持修繕工事's contract amount. No source consulted treats it as the
subcontract value.

For this crew the difference is the whole test. An insulation subcontract on a
¥30,000,000 refurbishment might itself be worth ¥2,000,000. Read the item's
sentence as "our contract", and the job qualifies. Read the clause, and it does
not. The pack has the same ambiguity, and so does the registry note.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Say whose contract: the price of the construction work
the prime contractor took from the client, not the insulation subcontract.
Flag that the crew usually cannot know that figure and must ask.

### 5. `01.2.04` and the pack — 施行規則第18条の2第2項, the anti-splitting rule, is missing

**What the source says.** 第18条の2第2項:
「建設工事を同一の者が二以上の契約に分割して請け負う場合においては、これを一の
契約で請け負つたものとみなして、前項第一号イの規定を適用する。ただし、正当な
理由に基づいて契約を分割したときは、この限りでない。」

Split contracts are added back together for the ¥5,000,000 test unless there
was a legitimate reason to split them. Confirmed in the verbatim text and in
the 吉田 commentary. Neither the item, the pack nor the registry note carries
it.

It matters here for a specific reason: this crew's jobs average about two
months and run across several buildings for the same client. Serial small
contracts is the ordinary shape of their work, and this is the paragraph that
says you cannot get under the limit by cutting a job in two.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Add 第2項 to the item, the pack and the registry note.

### 6. `01.2.04` — the condition list omits the document that has to be in the vehicle

**Claim.** "every one of the following to hold at once" — followed by five
conditions, presented as the complete test.

**What is missing.** A subcontractor carrying waste under this exception must
keep a copy of the written construction contract (or, where a framework
contract is used, the annex carrying the relevant terms) **in the transport
vehicle**. Kanagawa Prefecture's guidance states this directly:
運搬車に「契約書（基本契約書の場合は別紙を含む）の写し等の書面等の備え付け」が
必須, while a manifest is not required.

This was verified against prefectural guidance, not against primary text — the
requirement sits in the 産業廃棄物処理基準 in the 施行令, which was not in the
supplied verbatim file and was not fetched. It should be confirmed against
政令 before being written up.

It is also the single most actionable element of the whole test for a van-based
crew: it is the thing a roadside check asks for.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Fetch 廃棄物処理法施行令第6条 through the e-Gov API,
confirm the carriage-document requirement and its exact scope, and add it. If
it cannot be confirmed, say in the item that the condition list may not be
complete and name who to ask, rather than presenting five conditions as
exhaustive.

### 7. `01.2.04` and the pack — 第21条の3第4項 is omitted, and it governs the case the item raises

**What the source says.** 第21条の3第4項 is in the verbatim text the pack was
built from. Where a subcontractor entrusts the carriage or disposal of
construction waste to someone else, the subcontractor is deemed the 事業者 and
the waste its own for the purposes of the 委託基準 (第12条第5項–第7項),
第12条の3 (manifest) and the rest — subject to a stated exception.

The pack lists 第1項, 第2項 and 第3項 and stops. The item follows. But the item
explicitly raises the entrusting case in its own "What is not settled here" —
"whether this company's offcuts in practice go back to the yard in the van or
into the prime contractor's own skip" — and the answer to the third possibility
(calling a carrier yourself) is 第4項.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Add 第4項 to the pack, the registry note and the item, at
least to the extent of: if the subcontractor arranges the carriage rather than
carrying it itself or handing it to the prime contractor, a different and much
heavier set of duties attaches, and that is not a decision for a crew to make.

### 8. `01.2.04` — the content of the storage standard is invented in the act of declining to state it

**Claim.** "for the purpose of **storage**, the subcontractor is also treated
as a generator of the waste. **How it is held on site — kept segregated, kept
out of a passage, kept away from an ignition source** — is this trade's own
responsibility" — followed two paragraphs later by "What the storage standards
under 第12条第2項 actually require … is not carried here."

**What is wrong.** The item does the thing it says it is not doing. The three
examples given are not the 産業廃棄物保管基準: those are set by 施行規則第8条 and
run to an enclosure, a display board of a prescribed minimum size carrying
prescribed content, and measures against 飛散, 流出, 地下浸透, 悪臭 and vermin.
"Kept out of a passage" and "kept away from an ignition source" come from
安衛則第542条 and 要綱第19, elsewhere in the same item; "kept segregated" comes
from 1.3.9. None of the three is a storage standard, and the sentence's grammar
attaches all three to 第12条第2項.

The pack's section E is careful here — "this pack does not carry that detail,
so do not state it". The item states it anyway, by illustration.

**Severity: significant.** A reader who satisfies these three things believes
they have met 第12条第2項 and has not gone near it.

**Suggested action.** Either delete the three examples, or fetch 施行規則第8条
and state the real ones. Deleting is cheaper and loses nothing the item needs.

### 9. `01.2.04` and `01.2.02` — the item states the destination condition correctly and then leaves the reader believing the opposite

**Claim.** `01.2.04`: "the destination is a facility … in the **same prefecture
as the site, or a prefecture adjacent to it**, and one **the prime contractor
owns or otherwise has the right to use**". That is exactly right — 施行規則第18条の2第1項第2号ロ
limits the destination to a facility 元請業者が所有権を有するもの, or where it
does not own it, 使用する権原を有するもの. It is the element most often got
wrong and the item gets it right.

**What is wrong is the conclusion that is not drawn.** If the destination must
be the prime contractor's facility, then taking offcuts back to *your own
employer's yard* is not something the exception permits. Yet both items leave
that as an open, evenly-balanced question: `01.2.02` says "Whether offcuts and
packaging … go back to the yard in the van or into the prime contractor's own
skip is governed by law rather than by convenience — see `01.2.04` for which law
and what it turns on", and `01.2.04` says "which of the two actually happens on
a given job depends on the contract in place".

It does not depend only on the contract. One of the two options is outside the
exception unless the prime contractor happens to own or have the right to use
this company's yard, which is not the ordinary case.

A second point, smaller but the same shape: the anchor for "same or adjacent
prefecture" is 「当該廃棄物を生ずる**事業場**の所在地の属する都道府県」 — the site
generating the waste, not the company's base. Both the pack and `01.2.04`
present it as a question about a crew "based in Iwakuni". For Yamaguchi and
Hiroshima the answer happens to come out the same either way, which is exactly
why the wrong anchor will survive unnoticed until the geography changes.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** In `01.2.04`, draw the conclusion: under this route the
waste goes to the prime contractor's facility, so "back to our yard" is
generally not one of the two options. Correct the anchor to the site. Fix the
matching sentence in `01.2.02`.

### 10. `01.2.01` — 第552条 is cited for a scaffold, and scaffolds are 第563条

**Claim.** Common mistakes: "**Assuming a fallen handrail or a missing scaffold
panel is a temporary convenience rather than a barred area.** 第552条第2項第2号
requires that gap to be treated as no-entry the moment the rails come off."

**What the source says.** 第552条 governs 架設通路 — temporary walkways. Fall
protection on scaffolds is 第563条: 第563条第1項 requires a 作業床 on a scaffold
at 2 m or more, 第563条第3項 permits temporary removal of the 足場用墜落防止設備
under stated conditions, and 第563条第5項 requires immediate restoration. The
body of `01.2.01` uses 第552条 correctly, for a walkway. The Common mistakes
bullet extends the same citation to a scaffold, where a different article
applies.

The practical instruction survives — the parallel structure in 第563条 gives the
same answer — but the citation a reader would follow is wrong, and this project
treats a broken audit trail as a finding in its own right
(`reviews/ch01-factcheck.md` finding 12).

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Split the bullet, or restrict it to 架設通路 and cite
第563条 for the scaffold case. 第563条 is not in the pack and would need adding.

### 11. `01.2.02` and `01.2.04` — 第542条's 1.8 m is a headroom rule, used as a stacking-height rule

**Claim.** `01.2.02`: "**nothing may be placed within 1.8 m above the passage
surface**. A stack of pipe sections against a corridor wall, piled higher than
that, is not a tidiness question. It is that clause." `01.2.04` repeats it as
"the 1.8 m clearance above an indoor passage that piled material is not allowed
to breach".

**What the source says.** 第542条第3号: 「通路面から高さ一・八メートル以内に
**障害物を置かない**こと」. It prohibits placing an obstruction inside the 1.8 m
envelope **over the passage**. It is a headroom rule about the passage, not a
height limit on material stacked beside one. A 2 m stack set against the wall
outside the passage width does not engage 第3号 at all; what it engages is
第542条第1号 (width matched to use) and 第540条 (the passage kept effective) —
both of which `01.2.02` cites correctly in the next sentence.

Read the item's way, a crew concludes that a 1.7 m stack narrowing a corridor
to single file is compliant and a 1.9 m stack in an alcove is not. Both
conclusions are backwards.

The pack states the clause accurately. This is an item-level error, repeated
across two items.

**Severity: significant** — it is a number a reader will measure against.

**Suggested action.** Present 1.8 m as overhead clearance over the passage, and
make the width/effectiveness duties the ones that govern a stack beside a wall.

### 12. `01.2.03` — 第654条 does not relieve the crew's own employer, and 第552条第1項 is a prohibition on *using* a non-conforming walkway

**Claim.** "a walkway a crew is simply handed … is **somebody else's legal
responsibility to have built correctly, not a structure to inspect and repair
on the spot**."

**What the source says.** 第552条第1項 is addressed to the 事業者 — the crew's
own employer — and its operative words are 「…適合したものでなければ**使用しては
ならない**」. Not "must be built correctly by someone", but "must not be used
unless it conforms". 安衛法第32条第4項 adds a duty on the 請負人 to take the
measures corresponding to those the 注文者 takes under 第31条第1項.

So the duty is doubled, not transferred. 第654条 puts the building duty on the
注文者; 第552条第1項 independently forbids the crew's employer from using a
walkway that fails. "Not a structure to inspect" is the opposite of what the
first paragraph of the article requires.

The item's own next sentences recover most of the ground — a walkway that looks
wrong "is worth reporting to the supervisor rather than using carefully" — but
they sit in tension with the sentence above them, and the reader is told twice
that the responsibility is someone else's.

A related narrowing: the item calls the duty-holder "the party that ordered the
work". 安衛法第31条第1項 confines it to a 注文者 who 自ら行う the work and
supplies the 建設物等 — in practice the prime contractor, not the hospital that
owns the building. 第31条第2項 puts it on the first such 注文者 in the chain
where there are several. The item's phrasing, next to "working inside somebody
else's building", invites the reader to think of the building owner.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Rewrite so both duties are visible: the 注文者 must build
it to 第552条, and your own employer must not let you use it if it fails. Name
the prime contractor as the usual duty-holder.

### 13. `01.2.02` and `01.2.03` — the claim that 第540条–第542条 reach a borrowed hospital corridor is inference stated as scope

**Claim.** `01.2.03`: "they apply to **any workplace**, which is why they reach
a corridor inside an occupied building as much as a new-build site."
`01.2.02`: "it applies whether the passage belongs to a construction site or to
a hospital corridor the job is borrowing for the day."

**What the source says.** 第540条 is written about 作業場に通ずる場所及び作業場内
and duties to 設け and 保持 them; 第542条 is captioned 屋内に**設ける**通路. Both
attach to the 事業者 in respect of passages it provides. Whether they bind an
insulation subcontractor in respect of a hospital's own existing corridor, which
the subcontractor neither built nor controls, is a real question. The
administrative position may well be that they do, through 第540条's reach to
places leading to the 作業場 — but no source consulted here says so, and the
pack does not claim it either. The pack states the clauses neutrally; the reach
is the items' own addition.

The direction is safe — a reader who keeps a hospital corridor clear is not
harmed by the belief. But this is precisely the pack's own rule 1, "do not let
an inference wear the clothes of a cited rule", and it is the load-bearing
sentence in two items.

**Severity: significant** (sourcing practice; the practical advice is sound).

**Suggested action.** Mark the reach into a borrowed corridor as reasoning, not
as what the clause says. One sentence: the clauses are written about the
passages your employer provides, and treating a borrowed corridor the same way
is the safe reading, not a settled one.

### 14. `01.2.02` — a single tin of adhesive is presented as a breach of 要綱第19

**Claim.** Common mistakes: "**Storing adhesive or solvent wherever there is
space.** 第19 requires a dedicated, ventilated store marked 危険物 and 火気厳禁
with someone responsible for it. **A tin left in the general staging spot is a
routine failure of that requirement, not a rare one.**"

**What the source says.** 建築工事等編第19第1項 opens with 「作業場に危険物を
**貯蔵**する場合には、**関係法令等に従い**、適正に保管しなければならない」 and
only then requires the 危険物貯蔵所 for flammable paints, oils, other flammable
materials and cylinders. Two qualifiers the item drops: the clause is about
**storing** dangerous goods at the work area, and it operates 関係法令等の定める
ところにより — that is, through 消防法 and the municipal 少量危険物 ordinances,
which regulate by quantity against the 指定数量. A few litres of contact
adhesive carried in on the van and taken away again is a long way below any
threshold that triggers a 貯蔵所.

The body of the item is better than the bullet — it calls it "a question for the
supervisor". The bullet asserts a breach.

Telling a new worker that ordinary practice is a routine legal failure, when it
probably is not, costs credibility that the genuine findings in this section
need.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Keep the clause, drop the verdict. Say that the store is
what the clause requires where dangerous goods are stored at the work area, that
whether a day's worth of adhesive in the van reaches that point turns on
quantity thresholds in fire law that this item does not carry, and that
cylinders are the clear case.

### 15. All five items — every 要綱 clause is cited by bare number, and the same numbers are different clauses in the other volume

**What is wrong.** Not one of the five items names 建築工事等編. They cite 第1,
第14, 第16, 第17, 第18, 第19, 第21, 第22 and 第33 as though the number alone
identified the clause. The document at the registry URL contains both volumes.
From the full clause list of each:

| No. | 建築工事等編 (cited) | 土木工事編 (same number) |
|---|---|---|
| 第14 | 整理・整頓 | 整理整頓 — same subject, **different text** |
| 第16 | 粉塵対策 | 作業場の出入口 |
| 第17 | 適正な照明 | 型枠支保工、足場等の計画及び設計 |
| 第18 | 火災防止 | 建設資材等の運搬 |
| 第19 | 危険物貯蔵 | 足場等の設置・解体時の作業計画及び手順 |
| 第21 | 仮囲い、出入口 | 安全巡視 |
| 第22 | 建設資材等の運搬 | 作業場への工事車両の出入り等 |
| 第33 | 歩行者用通路の確保 | 道路の上空における橋梁架設等の作業 |

Six of the eight numbers cited land on an unrelated subject in the civil
volume. `01.2.02` cites 第19 for the dangerous-goods store, which in the civil
volume is scaffold work planning; `01.2.03` cites 第33 for pedestrian widths,
which in the civil volume is bridge erection over a road. Every clause was
checked and **every one is correctly taken from the building volume** — the
substance is right. What is missing is the label that lets anyone check.

**Severity: significant** (verification integrity, not fact).

**Suggested action.** Name the volume at first mention in each item. The pack
warned about this in general terms; the warning needs to become a habit in the
prose.

### 16. `01.2.03` — 第543条 governs the width of a passage between machines, not the gap between machines

**Claim.** "第543条 requires **at least 80 cm between machines**, or between a
machine and other equipment, and it is the clause that actually bites in a
crowded plant room."

**What the source says.** 「機械間又はこれと他の設備との間に**設ける通路**に
ついては、幅八十センチメートル以上のものとしなければならない」. The 80 cm is
the width of a passage provided between machines. It is not a general minimum
separation between plant items, and in an existing plant room the machines were
positioned by someone else years ago.

The practical warning — that scaffold left leaning in a plant room eats the
clearance a worker carrying a pipe section needs — is good and worth keeping.
It just is not what the number measures.

**Severity: minor.** In the safe direction, and the reader action is right.

**Suggested action.** Reword to "a passage provided between machines must be at
least 80 cm wide".

### 17. `01.2.01` — 第585条 is said to list seven categories and six are given, with 著しく暑熱 dropped

**Claim.** "第585条 lists **seven** categories where entry is barred with a
conspicuous notice: places handling large quantities of very hot or very cold
material; harmful light or ultrasound; carbon dioxide over 1.5 percent…"

**What the source says.** Seven items, and the item's list collapses 第1号 and
第2号 into one and loses half of each. 第1号 is 「多量の高熱物体を取り扱う場所
**又は著しく暑熱な場所**」 and 第2号 is 「多量の低温物体を取り扱う場所**又は
著しく寒冷な場所**」. A markedly hot place is a category in its own right,
independent of whether large quantities of hot objects are being handled.

For this trade specifically that is the wrong half to drop. A plant room around
uninsulated or stripped steam pipework is much more plausibly 著しく暑熱な場所
than 多量の高熱物体を取り扱う場所. The pack carries both halves correctly.

The item then says "the ones this trade actually meets are plant rooms handling
hot or cold equipment" — which is trade judgement, stated inside a paragraph
whose authority is the clause.

**Severity: minor**, but fix it while the item is open: the omitted half is the
one that applies.

**Suggested action.** Give both halves of 第1号 and 第2号, and either list seven
or stop saying seven.

### 18. `01.2.05` — the 第619条 scope limit reaches the right answer by an argument the clause does not support

**Claim.** "第619条 … sits in the hygiene standards, and it is **written for a
workplace an employer runs continuously — an office, a factory floor**. Reading
it as a rule about a two-day insulation job inside somebody else's hospital
would overstate what it covers; **nothing in it is written with a borrowed space
in mind**."

**What the source says.** 第619条 is addressed to 事業者 with no limitation as
to the permanence of the workplace or who owns it. It sits in 第三編 衛生基準
第七章 清潔, which applies to employers generally. Nothing in the article
restricts it to continuously-run premises; that restriction is the item's
inference, presented as a property of the clause.

Worse, the clause's own first item opens 「**日常行う清掃のほか**、大掃除を、
六月以内ごとに一回…」 — it takes daily cleaning as the baseline and adds a
six-monthly deep clean on top. The item uses this article to argue that the
regulations do not carry a daily-cleaning duty, when the article presupposes
one, and 第620条 next door imposes one on the worker (finding 2).

The conclusion — do not apply a six-monthly 大掃除 and pest survey to a two-day
hospital job — is sound and worth keeping. The reasoning is not.

**Severity: significant**, because it is one of the three deliberate scope
limits this review was called to test, and because the flaw runs in the
direction of telling the reader a duty does not reach them.

**Suggested action.** Recast: the six-monthly deep clean and pest survey are
aimed at premises an employer occupies over time and do not sensibly attach to
a two-day job in a borrowed building; the daily cleaning the article assumes,
and the worker's duty in 第620条, do.

### 19. `01.2.05` — 1.3.11's trigger is completion of the works, not completion of a part

**Claim.** "1.3.11 後片付け is a completion duty. It requires **the part of the
works concerned to be cleared up and cleaned once that part is finished**."

**What the source says.** 「工事の**完成に当たり**、当該工事に関する部分の後片付け
及び清掃を行う。」 The trigger is completion of the works; 当該工事に関する部分
identifies *what* is cleaned (the parts relating to that work), not *when*. The
item's rendering moves the trigger onto each part as it finishes, which is a
wider duty than the clause creates — and a wider duty than the item's own
headline conclusion, which is that 1.3.11 is a completion duty and not a daily
one.

**Severity: minor.** The conclusion the item draws is correct; the sentence it
draws it from is not. The pack renders this clause correctly, so the drift is
the item's.

**Suggested action.** "On completion of the works, the parts relating to that
work are cleared up and cleaned."

### 20. `01.2.01` — the plant-room sign explanation is inference in a clause's voice

**Claim.** "a permanent notice **required by fire-safety law**, aimed at the
building's occupants, on the exact door this trade is sent through" — grounded
on spec 1.7.4(1).

**What the source says.** 1.7.4(1) requires the contractor to install
「消防法（…）**等**に定めるところによる標識（危険物表示板、機械室等の出入口の
立入禁止表示、火気厳禁の標識等）」 as part of the works. Three gaps between that
and the item:

- 等 does real work. The clause does not say which law requires which sign, and
  a 立入禁止 display at a plant room door is not obviously a 消防法 requirement.
  "Required by fire-safety law" is more specific than the source.
- The clause is about signs the contractor installs on a job. The item uses it
  to explain the provenance of a sign already on the door of an existing
  hospital, which the clause does not address.
- "Permanent fixtures of the building … they do not go away when the job
  finishes" is a sound inference from "installed as part of the works" — but it
  is an inference, and the pack presented it as a nuance note rather than as
  clause content.

The reader action the item produces — confirm access through the supervisor
rather than reading the sign — is right, and is the best sentence in the item.

**Severity: minor.**

**Suggested action.** Say the specification requires such signs to be installed
as part of the works, under fire law and other law; mark the "so an existing
one is probably permanent" step as reasoning.

### 21. `01.2.01` — the item never answers its own question: why a rule from a company that does not employ you binds you

The item's subject is who can bar a place and whether the reader must obey. It
grounds the worker's own duty on 安衛則第585条第2項, which is correct but covers
only the seven hazardous categories. It does not reach the general case — a
corridor closed by the prime contractor, a rule announced at the morning
meeting by a company that does not employ you.

The pack had the answer and flagged it as such: 安衛法第26条 (workers must
observe what corresponds to the employer's measures under 第20条–第25条) and
第32条第6項・第7項 (the 請負人 and 作業従事者 must follow instructions given to
secure that those measures are carried out), which the pack glosses as "the
legal reason a site rule from a company that does not employ you is binding on
you". Neither article appears in any of the five items.

One caution if it is added: 第32条第7項 is about 指示 given to secure the
implementation of the listed statutory measures. It is not a blanket rule that
any instruction from any company on site is legally binding, and the pack's
gloss is slightly wider than the clause.

**Severity: significant** (omission of the item's own load-bearing rule).

**Suggested action.** Add 第26条 and 第32条第7項 to `01.2.01`, stated at the
width the clauses actually have.

## What could not be resolved

- **The document a subcontractor must carry in the vehicle** (finding 6). The
  requirement is stated by Kanagawa Prefecture's guidance and is consistent with
  the 産業廃棄物処理基準, but the 施行令 was not in the supplied verbatim file and
  was not fetched. Its exact scope — a copy of the contract, an annex, anything
  else — is open. Fetch 廃棄物処理法施行令第6条 through the e-Gov API before
  writing it up.

- **The content of the 産業廃棄物保管基準** (finding 8). 施行規則第8条 was not
  supplied and was not read. The item should not describe the standard until it
  has been, and this review cannot say more than that the three things the item
  currently offers are not it.

- **Whose 請負代金 the ¥5,000,000 threshold measures** (finding 4). The clause
  itself does not say in terms. Two independent commentaries render it as the
  prime contract amount and none suggests the subcontract. No 環境省 通知 was
  read directly. The safe course — say it is the prime contract and that the
  crew must ask — does not depend on resolving it, but the registry note should
  record which reading it is asserting and on what.

- **Whether 安衛則第540条 and 第542条 bind a subcontractor in respect of a
  building's own existing corridor** (finding 13). No administrative guidance
  was consulted. The items assert it; nothing read here confirms or denies it.

- **`mlit-kikai-shiyosho` clause 1.3.3(1)**, summarised in the pack, is not in
  the supplied verbatim extract and was not checked. No item uses it.

- **The specification was not re-downloaded for this review.** The clause text
  used here is the pypdf extraction supplied in the scratchpad, which
  `reviews/ch01-factcheck.md` established was taken from the registry URL and
  cross-checked against both editions. Clause numbering was taken on that basis
  rather than re-verified.

- **`01.2.05`'s claim that housekeeping is "almost the only thing" a prime
  contractor sees of a small subcontractor**, and the reputational argument
  built on it, is unsourced and unsourceable. It is presented as judgement
  rather than as fact and reads correctly as such. Recorded so nobody spends
  time looking for a source.

**Two things carried forward from this round.**

The pack was the single point of failure and it failed twice, in exactly the
way the review was designed to detect. Both errors were omissions rather than
misreadings — a condition left out of a list, an article never looked up — which
is the failure mode a pack invites, because a pack looks complete. The corrected
pack is preserved at `docs/source-packs/01.2-site-zones-and-rules.md` so the
next section does not rebuild it from scratch, with both corrections marked.

The review was given verbatim primary text and told the pack was under test.
That is what made the difference: every finding above rests on clause text, and
finding 25 records a case where search results alone would have "corrected" a
correct citation. Do it the same way next time.
