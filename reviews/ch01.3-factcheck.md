## The question this review was called to answer

Section 01.3 is the first section built under `docs/source-pack-protocol.md`,
which was written in response to the two errors that section 01.2's pack shipped
into five items. One pack fed all six items here. If it misread, all six agree
with each other and agreement proves nothing. So: **did the protocol control
anything, or is it ceremony?**

**The honest headline: the protocol works exactly as far as it is mechanised,
and its hand-done substitute did not work at all.**

Nothing critical was found. The clause attributions on the swept document are
sound, the section's most actionable passage — `01.3.06` on 建設業法第24条の4,
including its explicit rejection of the "delivery within twenty days" reading —
is **correct**, and `01.3.05`'s negative claim, the one this review was told to
try hardest to falsify, **survived a full-text search of 第1編 and is confirmed.**
Both of those are real results and both were earned by the pack.

But the split in where the findings fall is stark enough to be the finding:

| Where the clause came from | Findings |
|---|---|
| 建設業法 articles inside the `primary-sweep.mjs` window (±4 of 第24条の4, 第26条) | 1 (a dropped その他の / 監理技術者 widening — finding 3) |
| 建設業法 articles outside any sweep window (第2条, 第19条) | 2 (findings 1, 9) |
| Specification clauses the pack chose | 3 (findings 4, 5, 11) |
| Specification clauses nobody looked at (1.1.2, 1.2.1, 1.5.4(3), 1.6.2) | 5 (findings 6, 7, 8, 10, and part of 17) |
| Pack caution the items dropped | 1 (finding 12) |

Eleven of my sixteen substantive findings originate in clauses that no sweep, by
hand or by machine, ever put in front of the pack author. The pack's own account
of this is the sentence that fails:

> Not yet swept: the specification, which is a PDF … Its 第1編 section headings
> were read in full — 第1節 総則 through 第7節 完成図等 — **which is the same
> discipline done by hand.**

It is not the same discipline. Seven 節 headings is not forty-odd clause
captions, and it is precisely the granularity at which 1.1.2 用語の定義 — which
*defines* 一工程の施工, 工事関係図書, 監督職員の検査 and 特記, four terms these
items build on — and 1.2.1 実施工程表 — the clause governing a document
`01.3.01` glosses and 1.5.1 names — disappear into "総則" and "工事関係図書".
The full 第1編 read that produced this report took one tool call.

The second protocol claim, that qualifiers and COMPLETE/PARTIAL labels are
carried, is **half true**: the ただし in 第24条の4第2項 was carried and carried
well, the ただし in 1.2.2(3) and 1.5.4(2) were carried, the 努める/flat-duty

marked **COMPLETE** are not complete — 1.5.8 (dropped ただし, finding 4) and
第26条の4 (dropped その他の technical management and dropped 監理技術者,
finding 3) — which is the same failure mode as 01.2, in a pack that was
explicitly built to prevent it.

## Findings

### 1. `01.3.01`, `01.3.06` and the pack — 元請負人 is rendered as "the prime contractor", and the Act defines it far more widely

**Claim.** `01.3.06`: "建設業法第24条の4 (検査及び引渡し) … binds the **元請負人**
— the prime contractor — not the crew standing on site." `01.3.01`: "建設業法第24条の2
places a duty on the 元請負人 … This is a flat legal obligation on the prime
contractor". And, decisively, `01.3.01` on who holds the paperwork: "It sits with
the 元請負人 **or, more often on a job this size, with the mechanical services
contractor above the insulation subcontractor in the chain.**"

**What the source says.** 建設業法第2条第5項:

> 「元請負人」とは、下請契約における注文者で建設業者であるものをいい、「下請負人」とは、下請契約における請負人をいう。

The 元請負人 is **the orderer under any subcontract, at any tier, provided it
holds a licence.** It is not "the company holding the contract with the client" —
that is the 発注者's counterparty, and the Act calls the client's own contractor
nothing special in this article at all. A first-tier mechanical contractor that
orders insulation from this employer **is the 元請負人** for the purposes of
第24条の2 and 第24条の4, even though it is itself somebody's 下請負人.

`01.3.01`'s sentence sets the two in opposition — the 元請負人 *or* the mechanical
contractor above them — which teaches the reader the wrong mapping directly. And
`01.1.01`'s glossary reinforces it: 元請 is defined there as "the prime
contractor, the company holding the contract with the client", which is a fair
gloss of the everyday word 元請 but not of the Act's defined 元請負人.

**Why it matters, and why it is the dangerous direction.** This is the whole
practical payload of `01.3.06`. A reader — or the company owner reading over their
shoulder — who understands "the prime contractor" as the main contractor at the
top of the chain will conclude that the 20-day inspection duty and the
immediate-delivery duty are owed by somebody they have no contract with, and that
the mechanical contractor who actually engaged them owes them nothing. That is
**narrower than the Act**, in the item the brief identifies as the most
actionable in the section. The same error voids `01.3.01`'s "a right the crew's
employer holds": the right only reaches this employer under the wide definition.

The pack renders it "the prime" throughout and never carries 第2条第5項. The
article was three chapters outside any sweep window.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** In both items, gloss 元請負人 as *the contractor that
ordered your company's work — whoever engaged you, which need not be the main
contractor on the site* and say in one clause that the Act defines it that way at
第2条第5項. Rewrite `01.3.01`'s "or … the mechanical services contractor above"
sentence, which currently teaches the opposite. Add 第2条第5項 to the pack. Consider
a one-line note in `01.1.01`'s 元請 entry distinguishing the everyday word from
the Act's defined term.

### 2. `01.3.06` — specification 1.6.1 is made to gate 建設業法第24条の4, and they are two different notices between two different pairs of parties

**Claim.** Section heading: "**What 1.6.1 gates before any of this can start**".
Body: "the notice that the works are complete may be given to the 監督職員 … only
where **both** … Paperwork is not something that trails behind finished physical
work here. It is a condition of being allowed to say the work is finished at all,
and **it sits ahead of the whole chain that 建設業法第24条の4 then runs: no valid
notice, no twenty-day clock, no inspection, no delivery.**" Repeated in the
worker's-part list: "the 工事関係図書 clause 1.6.1 requires … is not something to
leave until later. It is a condition of the job being allowed to end at all."

**What the sources say.** They are unrelated notices.

- Specification 1.6.1(1): 「**契約書に基づく**工事を完成したときの通知は…監督職員に提出することができる」.
  Read with 1.1.2(ｱ) (「監督職員」とは、契約書に基づく監督職員…) and 1.1.2(ｲ)
  (「受注者等」とは、当該工事請負契約の受注者…), this is the **受注者's** notice to
  the **client's** supervising officer, under the main contract with the 発注者.
  1.1.2(ﾃ) confirms the resulting 工事検査 is performed by 発注者 or 検査職員.
- 建設業法第24条の4第1項: 「元請負人は、**下請負人から**その請け負つた建設工事が完成した旨の通知を受けたときは…」.
  This is the **subcontractor's** notice to **its own orderer**, under the
  subcontract. The 20-day clock runs from that receipt and from nothing else.

There is no dependency between them, and in the ordinary run of a job the
dependency is the other way round: subcontract packages are completed, inspected
and delivered up the chain *before* the main contractor is in a position to tell
the client the works are complete. The item states a precondition that does not
exist, and inverts the sequence.

The item elsewhere gets this exactly right — "Treating the specification as though
it also settled a subcontractor's handover would be stating a rule wider than any
clause in it actually gives" — which is what makes this passage a lapse rather
than a thesis. The pack warned against precisely this ("Do not present the
specification as governing a subcontractor's handover; it does not").

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Rewrite the section to say what is true: 1.6.1 governs the
*main* contractor's completion notice to the client and is the paperwork gate on
*that* event; 第24条の4 governs a completely separate notice this employer's
company gives to whoever engaged it. Keep the reader-facing moral — paperwork is
a gate, not a trailing chore — but stop the two chains being welded together. The
"no valid notice, no twenty-day clock" sentence should go.

### 3. `01.3.02`, `01.3.03` and the pack (marked COMPLETE) — 第26条の4 is stated narrower than the article, twice over

**Claim.** `01.3.02`: "建設業法第26条の4第2項 places a duty directly on the people
doing the work: they must follow the guidance the 主任技術者 gives as part of that
role — drawing up the plan for carrying out the work, controlling the programme,
controlling quality, and giving technical direction and supervision on site."
`01.3.03` repeats the same closed list. Both Common-mistakes bullets close it
further: "It binds a worker to the 主任技術者's guidance in that specific role —
the plan, the programme, quality control, technical direction — **not** to
instructions from anyone with more experience, whatever their actual role."

**What the source says.** 第26条の4第1項:

> 主任技術者**及び監理技術者**は、…当該建設工事の施工計画の作成、工程管理、品質管理**その他の技術上の管理**及び当該建設工事の施工に従事する者の技術上の指導監督の職務を誠実に行わなければならない。

第2項:

> 工事現場における建設工事の施工に従事する者は、**主任技術者又は監理技術者**がその職務として行う指導に従わなければならない。

Two limbs are lost.

- **「その他の技術上の管理」.** The three named heads are *examples* of technical
  management; the 職務 is technical management generally, plus technical direction
  and supervision. Presenting the four as an exhaustive list makes the duty
  narrower than the article. The protocol's own rule 2 names 等 as the token that
  widens a rule; その他の does the identical work and is on neither the protocol's
  list nor `primary-sweep.mjs`'s `QUALIFIERS` array.
- **「又は監理技術者」.** The duty to follow guidance runs to the 監理技術者 as much
  as to the 主任技術者. On the larger public jobs these items are otherwise
  describing — the ones with a 監督職員 and a 総合試運転調整 — a 監理技術者 is
  exactly who is present. Neither item mentions the word.

The damage is done by the Common-mistakes bullets: a reader taught "not
instructions from anyone with more experience, **whatever their actual role**"
plus a four-item closed list has been handed a test they will apply, and it will
return the wrong answer for a 監理技術者's instruction and for any technical
instruction outside the four heads.

The pack marks 第26条の4 **COMPLETE** and carries the same two losses.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** In both items, add "and other technical management of the
work" to the list and say the duty runs to the 主任技術者 **or 監理技術者**. Keep
the correct and valuable point that the duty is role-bound rather than
seniority-bound; that reading is right and is well made. Fix the pack and remove
the COMPLETE label or earn it.

### 4. `01.3.02`, `01.3.04` and the pack (marked COMPLETE) — specification 1.5.8's ただし is missing

**Claim.** `01.3.02`: "Clause 1.5.8 of the specification requires the 監督職員 to
attend the work in several listed cases…". `01.3.04`: "Clause 1.5.8 **requires**
that attendance in five listed cases…" and, built on it, "A run started and
finished without that question being raised cannot be un-covered afterward — the
chance is genuinely gone, not merely inconvenient to recover."

**What the source says.** 1.5.8(1):

> 次の場合は、監督職員の立会いを受ける。**ただし、これによることが困難な場合は、別に指示を受ける。**

The five cases are all correctly listed in both items — that part is right, and
`01.3.04` lists all five including the two the pack flagged as mattering most.
What is missing is the proviso: where attendance is difficult, the contractor
takes separate instruction instead. The rule as the items state it is
**unconditional**, which is wider than the clause.

It matters here more than a dropped proviso usually would, because `01.3.04`
constructs a piece of reader guidance on the absence of an alternative: raise it
before you wrap, because afterwards nothing can be done. The clause's own answer
to "attendance is not practical on this run" is 別に指示を受ける — ask for a
different instruction — which is a route the item tells the reader does not exist.

The pack marks 1.5.8 **COMPLETE** and does not carry the ただし. This is the
same class of failure as 01.2's dropped parenthetical, in the pack written to
prevent it.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Add the proviso to both items — one clause is enough: "where
attendance is not practicable, the clause has the 監督職員 give a separate
instruction instead, which is a reason to ask rather than to assume nothing can
be done." Fix the pack; the COMPLETE label on 1.5.8 is false.

### 5. `01.3.02` — 1.5.8 is described as a duty on the 監督職員, and it is a duty on the contractor

**Claim.** "Clause 1.5.8 of the specification **requires the 監督職員 to attend**
the work in several listed cases…"

**What the source says.** 「監督職員の立会いを**受ける**」 — the obligation is on
the party performing the work to *receive* the attendance, and 1.5.8(2) puts the
duty to request it, in good time, on the same party, with the 監督職員 directing
when. 1.1.1(1) makes this general: everything in the specification is performed
「受注者の責任において」.

The item inverts who is bound. `01.3.04` handles it correctly ("Attendance under
this clause has to be requested in good time, and it is the contractor's job to
provide whatever that attendance actually needs"), so the two items disagree with
each other on the same clause. The pack's phrasing — "The 監督職員 attends where:
…" — is where the inversion came from, and the protocol's rule 2 names "who the
duty binds" as the thing most often missed.

Practical consequence: a reader told the 監督職員 is required to attend will wait
to be visited. The clause requires their side to ask.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Reword `01.3.02` to match `01.3.04`: the clause requires the
contractor's side to receive — and to request — the 監督職員's attendance in the
listed cases. One sentence.

### 6. `01.3.03`, `01.3.04` — 一工程 is a defined term in the specification, and neither the glossary nor the items use the definition

**Claim.** `01.3.03` glossary: "一工程 … a defined stage of the work that gets
confirmed as it is completed, rather than only at the end of the job."
`01.3.04`: "it happens at the end of every stage the job is divided into".

**What the source says.** 1.1.2(ﾂ), in the 用語の定義 clause of the same 第1編:

> 「一工程の施工」とは、施工の工程において、**同一の材料を用い、同一の施工方法により**作業が行われる場合で、**監督職員の承諾を受けた**ものをいう。

Three elements, none of which is "a stage the job is divided into": same material,
same construction method, and **approved by the 監督職員**. That definition is what
makes 1.5.4(2)'s sampling rule intelligible — sampling extends to parts built with
「同じ機材及び工法」, i.e. the same test the 一工程 definition applies.

The items' looser reading is not harmless. `01.3.04` tells a reader that
confirmation "happens at the end of every stage the job is divided into"; on the
clause, a 一工程 is a unit the 監督職員 has consented to, so on a job where no such
consent exists there may be no 一工程 at all — which is very likely the answer to
the open question all three items keep raising ("whether the 監督職員's consent … is
a real, visible event on a job of this scale"). The definition clause was one
paragraph away from answering the question the section asks four times.

Both items also carry the second 承諾 (of the *person* who confirms, from 1.5.3)
and neither carries the first (of the 一工程 itself, from 1.1.2(ﾂ)).

The pack never extracted 1.1.2.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Correct the glossary entry to the specification's own
definition — same material, same method, and consented to by the 監督職員 — and
add a sentence in `01.3.03` saying so. It also lets `01.3.04` sharpen its open
question into a specific one worth asking a supervisor: *has the 監督職員
consented to a 一工程 on this package at all?*

### 7. `01.3.04` — the 工事関係図書 glossary entry is not what the specification defines, and does not stand alone

**Claim.** Frontmatter term: "工事関係図書 … the set of construction-related
documents the design documents require to be complete before the works can be
declared finished."

**What the source says.** 1.1.2(ｾ):

> 「工事関係図書」とは、実施工程表、施工計画書、施工図等、工事写真その他これらに類する施工、試験等の報告及び記録に関する図書をいう。

It is a **class of documents** — the programme, the plan, the shop drawings, site
photographs, and the reports and records of construction and testing. It is not
defined by the completion gate; 1.6.1(1)(ｲ) merely *uses* it, requiring the
工事関係図書 that the design documents call for to be complete.

The entry defines a term by one clause's use of it. Extracted into a glossary and
read on its own — which is how the entry will be met — it tells a reader that
工事関係図書 means "the documents needed before completion can be declared", which
is circular and wrong, and gives them no way to recognise that the 施工図 in their
hand and the photographs in `01.9.03` *are* 工事関係図書. This is the same failure
mode the 契約不適合責任 entry was tightened for once already.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Replace the entry with the specification's own class
definition (programme, plan, shop drawings, site photographs and the related
construction and test records), and leave the completion-gate point in the body
where it belongs.

### 8. `01.3.01` — the 実施工程表 / 工程表 distinction is a reconstruction, and specification 1.2.1 governs the document and was never read

**Claim.** "実施工程表 is worth distinguishing from the 工程表 that 01.1.01 already
covers: a 工程表 is the schedule with dates fixed against it, and the **実施工程表
is the version the crew is actually working to on the ground, which can move as
the job runs** even though the underlying dates were fixed earlier." Frontmatter:
"実施工程表 … the working programme actually followed to carry out the works."

**What the source says.** 1.2.1, the first clause of 第2節 and immediately before
the 1.2.2 the item does cite:

> (1) 工事の着手に先立ち、実施工程表を作成し、**監督職員の承諾を受ける。**
> (3) 契約書に基づく条件変更等により、実施工程表を変更する必要が生じた場合は、…直ちに変更し、当該部分の施工に先立ち、**監督職員の承諾を受ける。**
> (4) (3)によるほか、…変更する必要が生じた場合は、**監督職員に報告する**とともに、…適切な措置を講ずる。
> (5) 監督職員の指示を受けた場合は、実施工程表の補足として、週間工程表、月間工程表、工種別工程表等を作成し、監督職員に提出する。

The 実施工程表 is a controlled document: produced before 着手, **approved** by the
監督職員, and re-approved or at minimum reported when it changes. The documents
closest to "the version the crew is actually working to" are the weekly, monthly
and per-trade programmes at 1.2.1(5), which are supplements produced on
instruction. The item's distinction is plausible, is stated flatly and without
hedge in an item labelled `standard-practice`, rests on no source, and points
away from the clause: a reader taught the 実施工程表 "can move as the job runs"
has been taught the opposite of a document that needs consent to move.

1.2.1 was never in the pack, despite 1.5.1 — which the pack quotes and three items
cite — naming 実施工程表 in its list.

**Severity: significant.**

**Suggested action.** Cite 1.2.1 and state what it says: produced before 着手,
approved by the 監督職員, and changes go back for approval or report. If a
distinction from 工程表 is worth keeping, ground it in 1.2.1(5)'s weekly/monthly
supplements rather than in an invented formal/informal split. Add 1.2.1 to the
pack.

### 9. `01.3.05` — "not something carried by … the Construction Business Act" is too absolute; 第19条第1項 carries two directly relevant items

**Claim.** "The rules that actually govern putting bad work right — 契約不適合責任
under the Civil Code, and whatever the subcontract … says about defects — are
contract law, **not something carried by the specification or the Construction
Business Act.**" Glossary: "契約不適合責任 … liability for work that does not
conform to the contract, which is where a demand for rework actually comes from;
the Civil Code provisions behind it are not among this project's sources."

**What the source says.** 建設業法第19条第1項 — an article the item's own cited
source contains — requires the parties to a construction contract to record, in a
signed written document exchanged between them:

> 十一　注文者が工事の全部又は一部の完成を確認するための**検査の時期及び方法並びに引渡しの時期**
> 十三　工事の目的物が**種類又は品質に関して契約の内容に適合しない場合におけるその不適合を担保すべき責任**又は当該責任の履行に関して講ずべき保証保険契約の締結その他の措置に関する定めをするときは、その内容

So the Act does not *create* 契約不適合責任 — the item is right about that — but it
is not silent on it either. It names the liability in almost the words the
glossary reaches for, and it requires the subcontract to record the term in
writing where one is agreed, plus (第2項) any later variation of it. That is a
real, citable, reader-useful fact sitting in a registry source the item already
cites: *your company's subcontract is required by law to say this in writing, so
there is a document to go and look at.*

第19条第1項第11号 is the same point for `01.3.06`: the subcontract is required to
state the inspection timing and method and the delivery time — which is exactly
the 特約 machinery 第24条の4第2項's proviso presupposes, and which turns
`01.3.06`'s "the governing document is the subcontract this employer's company has
signed" from an assertion into something with a legal hook.

The sweep window (±4 around 第24条の4 and 第26条) could not reach 第19条 by

### 10. `01.3.04` — 1.6.2 技術検査 is a fifth kind of inspection, in the same 節, and is not mentioned

**Claim.** "on this trade it covers **at least four** different things that happen
at different times, are carried out by different people, and follow from different
clauses". The item's whole structure is a four-way taxonomy.

**What the source says.** 1.6.2, the next clause after the 1.6.1 the item ends on,
in 第6節 工事検査**及び技術検査**:

> (1) 公共工事の品質確保の促進に関する法律に基づく技術検査を行う時期は、次による。
> (ｱ) 1.6.1「工事検査」の(1)及び(2)に示す工事検査を行うとき。
> (ｲ) **工事施工途中における技術検査（中間技術検査）**の実施回数及び実施する段階が**特記**された場合、その実施する段階に到達したとき。
> (ｳ) 発注者が特に必要と認めたとき。

A distinct inspection, by a different statute, performed by the 発注者 or 検査職員,
and — the part that reaches a crew — capable of happening **mid-construction**
where the job specifies it. 1.1.2(ﾄ) defines it separately from 工事検査.

The item's "at least four" hedge means this is an omission and not a false
statement, and I record it as such. But an item whose thesis is "a worker's actual
job is knowing which one a given run is waiting for" gives the reader a closed set
of four to sort into, and the fifth can arrive in the middle of the job.

The same applies more mildly to the materials inspection at 1.4.4(1), which
`01.3.05` relies on and `01.3.04`'s taxonomy does not include.

**Severity: minor.**

**Suggested action.** Add a short fifth entry, or a paragraph after the four,
noting 1.6.2 技術検査 — different statute, client-side, and possibly mid-job where
特記 says so — and a line pointing at 1.4.4's materials inspection as a sixth thing
called "inspection". Add 1.6.2 to the pack.

## Did the protocol work?

**Yes on the swept document, no on the unswept one, and the pack's claim to have
swept the unswept one by hand is the single thing most worth fixing.**

**Rule 1, sweep neighbourhoods (建設業法): worked, and produced content.** The
sweep was really run, its output really changed the pack, and the three articles
it surfaced all reached the reader in usable form:

- 第24条の2 is the whole "A right the crew's company has" section of `01.3.01`,
  and is stated correctly, as a flat duty, with the right limit on who can invoke
  it. Nobody choosing articles from expectation writes an item around it.
- The ただし in 第24条の4第2項 is the strongest passage in the section — `01.3.06`
  not only carries it but explicitly refutes the wrong reading of it, which is
  what a carried qualifier is *for*.
- 第26条の4第2項, a duty on the reader personally, reached two items.

That is three real gains against zero selection failures inside the sweep window.
Set against 01.2, where the equivalent gap (第620条) inverted the conclusion of an
entire item, this is not ceremony.

**Rule 1, applied by hand to the specification: failed, and the failure was
predicted by its own post-mortem.** The pack says reading 第1編's seven 節 headings
is "the same discipline done by hand". Reading the whole 第1編 — one tool call,
590 lines — produced findings 6, 7, 8, 10 and part of 17, from four clauses
(1.1.2, 1.2.1, 1.5.4(3), 1.6.2) that no heading-level read could ever surface.
Two of the four sit *adjacent* to clauses the pack took: 1.2.1 is immediately
before 1.2.2, and 1.6.2 immediately after 1.6.1. That is 01.2's error one,
reproduced exactly, in the section written to prevent it. The pack's honesty about
not having swept mechanically is real and creditable; the equivalence claim
attached to it is what turned an admitted gap into an unexamined one.

**A structural gap the protocol does not currently name:** `primary-sweep.mjs`
sweeps *neighbours by number*, and both of my 建設業法 findings outside the window
(第2条 definitions, 第19条 mandatory contract terms) are **definition and
foundation articles**, which are never numerically adjacent to the operative
article that uses them. The same class of miss hit the specification: 1.1.2
用語の定義 defines four terms these items use and is thirty clauses away from the
ones cited. Neighbourhood sweeping cannot find definitions. The protocol needs a
second, separate rule — *read the definition clause of any document you cite,
always* — and it would have caught findings 1, 6 and 7 outright.

**Rule 2, carry the qualifiers: mixed.** Carried correctly: ただし in 1.2.2(3),
1.5.4(2), 第24条の4第2項; 特記 conditionality in 1.5.2 and 1.7.1 (both then carried
faithfully into the items, and `01.3.06` makes the 特記 default the centre of a
whole subsection — a direct protocol win); 努める versus flat duty in 第24条の7 and
第25条の27. Dropped: the ただし in 1.5.8(1); その他の技術上の管理 in 第26条の4第1項;
「又は監理技術者」 in 第26条の4第2項; the who-is-bound direction of 1.5.8. Note that
`QUALIFIERS` in the sweep tool contains 等 but not **その他の**, which does identical
widening work and is far more common in statute — that is a one-line fix with
real yield.

**Rule 3, COMPLETE/PARTIAL: the labels are not trustworthy yet.** Two clauses
marked COMPLETE are not (1.5.8, 第26条の4). Two clauses carry no label at all
(1.5.4, 1.2.3). The PARTIAL labels, by contrast, were honest and were *obeyed* —
`01.3.01` and `01.3.03` both tell the reader their accounts of 1.2.2 and 1.2.4 are
incomplete, which is exactly the behaviour rule 3 was written to produce, and
which 01.2.04 did not manage. So the mechanism works; the labelling discipline
does not yet.

**Rule 4, reconcile as a separate pass: no evidence it happened.** The two false
COMPLETE labels are what a reconciliation pass exists to catch — both are visible
by reading the clause once more with only the question "what does the pack not
say?" in mind. The pack records no reconciliation.

**Rule 6, give the reviewer primary text and name the pack as suspect: worked,
and is the reason this report exists.** Every finding above except 12, 14 and 16
was found by reading a document rather than the pack. Had this review been handed
the pack, it would have confirmed all six items and reported clean — the items are
faithful to the pack almost everywhere. One caution for next time: the verbatim
extract supplied was assembled around the clauses the items ended up citing, with
three clauses added *after* the items were written. That is the same selection
bias one level down. It did not bite here because the raw PDF text and XML were
beside it and were read in full, which is exactly what rule 5 is for — but the
extract should be built from the *source's structural unit*, not from the citation
list, or the reviewer inherits the pack's blind spot in a new wrapper.

**Net.** Section 01.2 shipped two critical errors; section 01.3 shipped none, and
its most actionable statutory passage is correct in a way that would be very
unlikely without a pack. The protocol is doing real work. What it is not yet doing
is anything at all about documents it cannot read mechanically, and 40 more
sections of a PDF specification are exactly that case.

**Three changes I would make before the next section:**

1. Add a rule: **read the 用語の定義 / 第2条 definition clause of every document
   you cite, and carry every defined term the pack uses.** This is the highest-yield
   single change available — three of my findings die on it.
2. Extend `primary-sweep.mjs` to take a clause list for a text file (`--file
   mlit-kikai.txt --unit "第1編"`), printing every clause number and heading in the
   unit with the cited ones marked. The 第1編 is 590 lines of plain text; this is a
   thirty-line function and it converts "the same discipline done by hand" from a
   claim into a printout. Failing that, delete the equivalence claim from the pack
   template and make an unswept document a stated limitation on every item that
   rests on it.
3. Add **その他** to `QUALIFIERS`, and change the caption fallback string so the
   tool stops asserting that caption-less articles are deleted.

## What could not be resolved

- **建設業法施行令第30条** (特定専門工事 = 型枠工事 and 鉄筋工事, ¥40,000,000
  ceiling), used to judge whether the 第26条の3 dismissal survives on the merits,
  was verified only against practitioner commentary on the open web. The 施行令
  text was not supplied and was not pulled. The conclusion — that 第26条の3 cannot
  reach 熱絶縁工事 — should be re-checked against the 政令 before it is written into
  the pack as a reason.
- **The one-day question in 「二十日を経過した日」.** Japanese legal drafting
  convention distinguishes 「経過する日」 (the last day of the period) from
  「経過した日」 (the day after it), which would put 第24条の4第2項's outer limit on
  day 21 rather than day 20. Professional commentary uniformly paraphrases the
  proviso as 「20日以内」, as the item does. I could not find an authoritative
  statement resolving the convention *for this article*, and the difference cannot
  affect this readership. Recorded, not raised as a finding.
- **Whether the 令和7年版 and the 令和7年版（修補版） differ in any clause used
  here.** Only the 修補版 was available. Finding 17 is about the registry naming
  the wrong print, not about a known textual difference.
- **Whether the section passes `npm run validate`.** No shell was available. The
  properties checkable by reading the validator and grepping — body-link filenames
  against taxonomy paths, canonical terminology, Japanese terms declared somewhere,
  no first person, no brief-leak phrases, no reproduced clause text — are all
  clean. `01.3.04` will trip the advisory >1,200-words-on-one-source **warning**;
  keeping one source is the correct choice and the warning should be accepted, not
  silenced.
- **Whether any of the pack's five "What nobody has established" questions have
  since been answered by the employer.** All five are still carried as open
  questions in the items, correctly and with "ask your supervisor" attached. If the
  owner can answer even one — particularly whether a 監督職員's 承諾 of a 一工程 or
  of a confirming person is ever a real event on a package this size — three items
  lose their largest hedge and finding 6 becomes concrete guidance.
