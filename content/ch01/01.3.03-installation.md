# Installation

## What it is

Installation is the work itself — cutting, fitting, wrapping and finishing
insulation on the pipe or duct run that has already been confirmed ready in
[01.3.02](../ch01/01.3.02-preparation.md). It is where most of a working life
in this trade actually happens, and it is easy to picture as just the craft:
hands, tools, material, a run of pipe. It is not only that. While the work
runs, it stays bound to the paper that was agreed before it started, it gets
divided into stages that are confirmed as they finish rather than only at the
very end, and at least one person on a job of any consequence is expected to
be certified in the trade and to be guiding the others while they work.

## Why it matters on the job

The reason this deserves separating from preparation is that preparation
happens once for a given run and installation is where a worker spends hours,
not minutes. Small decisions accumulate over that time — whether to follow
the drawing exactly when the pipe in front of a worker does not sit where the
drawing says it does, whether to keep going past the point a stage was
supposed to be checked, whether to treat the person working next to them as
someone whose word settles a question. Getting those decisions right, one at
a time, over a whole shift, is most of what separates a crew that is trusted
with harder work from one that is not.

No source consulted here describes step-by-step how insulation is physically
applied to a given run — that belongs to the trade chapters on materials and
technique, not to this item. What belongs here is the frame installation sits
inside: what governs it, what gets confirmed while it runs, and who a worker
answers to when something does not match.

## In detail

### The paper still governs, and it does not lose to what is in front of you

Specification clause 1.5.1, in Part 1 of the standard specification for
mechanical services work on public building projects — the general common
provisions that sit ahead of the trade-specific parts, cited from there
because the specification reuses clause numbers across its later parts — was
introduced in [01.3.01](../ch01/01.3.01-mobilization.md) as the spine of the
whole sequence: the work is carried out on the basis of the design documents,
the 実施工程表, the 施工計画書 and the 施工図. [01.3.02](../ch01/01.3.02-preparation.md)
used the same clause to establish that the drawing governing a run is a
controlled document that has to be current before a tool is picked up. What
belongs here is the consequence once cutting has actually started: that
governing relationship does not pause once the work is under way, and it does
not reverse just because the physical run in front of a worker looks
different from what the drawing shows.

A pipe run that has moved, a support that was fixed somewhere other than the
施工図 shows, a duct that turns a corner the drawing does not — none of that
means the drawing has been overtaken by reality and can now be ignored. It
means the drawing and the reality disagree, and clause 1.5.1 puts the design
documents in charge of what gets built, not whichever version happens to be
easier to see with the eyes on a given morning. [04.5.04](../ch04/04.5.04-never-guessing-from-outdated-drawings.md)
covers the failure mode of working from an out-of-date drawing directly, and
the instruction that belongs here is the same one stated from the installer's
side: a mismatch is a reason to stop and ask, through the supervisor, not a
judgment call a worker resolves alone by picking whichever version looks more
convenient to build to.

### 技能士 — the qualification this trade expects, not a bonus

Specification clause 1.5.2 defines 技能士, for the purposes of this
specification, as a holder of 一級技能士 — first-grade trade skill
certification — or the equivalent single-grade qualification recognized under
Japan's vocational training law. Which trades and which specific operations
the requirement applies to is stated separately for each job. Where it does
apply, the clause requires that at least one such person actually perform the
work themselves and give work guidance to the others carrying it out, in
order to raise the quality of what gets built — and evidence of that
person's qualification is submitted to the 監督職員, the client's supervising
officer on public work.

This is not a distant administrative detail. Partner companies working
alongside this employer hold the trade's own top-grade certification —
一級熱絶縁施工技能士, the first-grade national qualification for thermal
insulation installation — as a matter of course, not as something unusual to
mention. On a job where clause 1.5.2 applies, the person actually guiding a
crew's work on the ground is very often that certified person, and the
guidance they give is not simply seniority; it is the role the specification
itself expects someone to be filling. That certification is years away for a
worker newly arrived in the trade, and studying toward it is not what this
item covers. What is worth carrying from the first weeks on the job is
straightforward: this is not an optional credential some workers happen to
collect. It is the qualification the trade is built around, and the
supervisor or senior worker directing the detail of the work on a job of any
size is, more often than not, someone who holds it.

### 一工程 — the work is confirmed as it goes, not only at the end

Specification clause 1.5.3 sets out that on completing a 一工程 — a defined
stage of the work, not the whole job — or when the 監督職員 gives an
instruction during it, the work has to be confirmed against the design
documents and reported to the 監督職員 in good time. That confirmation and
that report are made by a person the 監督職員 has given consent to in
advance, not by whoever happens to be free to do it.

What that means for a worker actually doing the installing is that "finished"
has a meaning that arrives repeatedly through the job, not just once at the
very end. A run of pipe insulated to a given point, a section of duct
completed to a joint, is not simply left for someone to eventually notice —
it is a point where the work is meant to be checked against the drawing
before the crew moves on to the next stage. What actually counts as one
一工程 on a job this size, and how visibly that confirmation happens in
practice, is not something a worker can assume from the clause alone; the
inspection that follows from this confirmation, and what a crew does around
it, is covered in [01.3.04](../ch01/01.3.04-inspection.md) rather than here.

### Records kept while the work is running, not only after

Specification clause 1.2.4(4) requires records, site photographs and samples
to be kept in several situations: where a confirmation required by the
design documents was carried out, where a 一工程 was completed, where the
監督職員 instructs, and — the case that matters most for this trade — **where
work is being done that becomes concealed as the job proceeds, or that a
later visual inspection cannot easily reach.** This account does not cover
the whole of clause 1.2.4; the parts carried here are the ones bearing
directly on installation, and a reader relying on the clause for anything
beyond that should not assume this is the complete text.

Insulation is exactly the kind of work that clause describes: once it is
wrapped, the joint, the weld and the support underneath it are gone from
view. [01.9.03](../ch01/01.9.03-photographing-work-that-will-be-hidden.md)
covers how that work actually gets photographed on this employer's jobs;
what belongs here is the reason it happens while installation is still
running rather than afterward — once the wrap goes on, the chance to record
what is underneath it is gone for good.

### The duty already established, applied here

建設業法第26条の4第2項 — introduced and cross-referenced by
[01.3.02](../ch01/01.3.02-preparation.md) — binds the people doing the work
to follow the guidance the 主任技術者 gives in that specific role: drawing up
the plan for the work, controlling the programme, controlling quality, and
giving technical direction and supervision on site. That duty is stated at
the width the article actually gives it, and it is not restated here beyond
noting that installation is where it is exercised most often — a
correction to how a joint is being wrapped, an instruction to redo a section
that does not match the drawing, is very often this specific legal role in
practice rather than simple seniority.

### Practice: a sequence someone else set, and what happens when the run does not match the drawing

None of the specification clauses above say anything about the order tasks
happen in on a given day, and that order is usually not something a crew
decides for itself. The wider programme — the 実施工程表 covered in
[01.3.01](../ch01/01.3.01-mobilization.md) — is set above the crew, and
installation on a given day generally means working through a sequence that
has already been fixed: which run gets insulated first, which section has to
wait because another trade has not finished its own work there yet. That is
trade practice rather than a clause with a figure in it, and it is not
addressed by anything cited here beyond the general fact that the programme
governs the order of the job.

Stopping to ask rather than guessing at a mismatch, the point clause 1.5.1
was used to establish above, is straightforward to state and less
straightforward to live inside for a whole shift, because stopping costs
time on a job already running to a fixed programme. How a mismatch actually
gets raised on a job this size — whether it goes through the supervisor
directly, whether it waits for the next scheduled 一工程 confirmation, or
whether it is raised on the spot with whoever is nearby from the mechanical
contractor — is not established by anything consulted here. It is a
reasonable question to put to a supervisor directly rather than to guess an
answer to.

### Practice: other trades working around the same space

Occupied hospitals, schools and offices, and new-build sites alike, put this
trade's work next to plumbing, electrical, fire protection and general
construction work happening in the same space at the same time.
[01.6.01](../ch01/01.6.01-who-else-is-in-a-services-zone-and-what-they-are-doing.md) through
[01.6.05](../ch01/01.6.05-raising-a-clash-rather-than-working-around-it.md) cover how that shared space is
actually managed and are not restated here; what belongs to installation is
the plain fact that a run being insulated is rarely the only thing happening
in that room, and working around other trades is part of the job rather than
an interruption to it.

### Practice: who is actually checking the work on a small crew

On a six-person company, the person confirming that a stage of installation
matches the drawing is very often the person working immediately alongside
the crew that day, not someone arriving separately for the purpose. That
makes the working relationship on site different from a large contractor
where checking is a distinct, later step done by someone unfamiliar with the
run. Nothing consulted here describes this arrangement formally — it follows
from the shape of a small crew rather than from a clause — and it is
recorded here as trade practice, not as something clause 1.5.3 itself
states.

### What is not settled here

Who actually signs off a completed 一工程 on an insulation package this
small, and whether the 監督職員's consent to that person under clause 1.5.3
is a real, visible event on a job of this scale, is not addressed by
anything consulted here. On a large project the confirming person and the
主任技術者 giving guidance under 建設業法第26条の4第2項 might plausibly be the
same person; whether that is also true on a job this size, or whether the
confirmation is something the mechanical contractor above the insulation
subcontractor absorbs into its own process, is not established here either.
Both are reasonable questions for the crew's own supervisor or the company
owner, not something a specification clause answers on its own.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating a run that has moved from the drawing as the drawing being wrong.** Clause 1.5.1 puts the design documents in charge of what gets built. A mismatch between the run in front of a worker and the 施工図 is a reason to stop and ask through the supervisor, not a signal to build to whatever is easier to see.
- **Assuming certification is optional in this trade.** Clause 1.5.2 requires that where it applies, a certified 技能士 actually perform the work and guide others doing it. Partner companies around this employer hold 一級熱絶縁施工技能士 as standard practice, not as something unusual — it is the expected path in this trade, not an optional extra.
- **Waiting until the whole job is finished to think about confirmation.** Clause 1.5.3 confirms work at the end of each 一工程, not only at the very end of the job. Treating every stage as provisional until a final walk-through misses the point of dividing the work into stages at all.
- **Wrapping a run without thinking about what gets recorded first.** Clause 1.2.4(4) requires records and photographs of work that becomes concealed as the job proceeds. Once insulation goes on, whatever was underneath it is gone from view for the rest of the job's life.
- **Reading 建設業法第26条の4第2項 as obedience to anyone senior on site.** It binds a worker to the 主任技術者's guidance in that specific role — the plan, the programme, quality control, technical direction — not to instructions from anyone with more experience, whatever their actual role.
